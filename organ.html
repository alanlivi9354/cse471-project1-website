How it works

The organ adds nine sine waves for the tonewheels. The drawbar order is 16', 5 1/3', 8', 4', 2 2/3', 2', 1 3/5', 1 1/3', and 1'. Following the handout's 16-foot reference, their frequency ratios are 1, 3, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 16.

The drawbars field has nine settings from 0 to 8. Zero turns a drawbar off. Eight is full volume, and each step down lowers it by 3 dB. The program adds the waves and divides by the drawbar amplitude sum, with a minimum divisor of 1. Frequencies at or above half the sample rate are left out.

attack and release control the start and end of the note. vibrato sets the pitch-change depth as a fraction of the frequency. It runs at 5.5 Hz. All organ notes use the song clock, so their vibrato stays in sync.

Grading elements

ElementWhere to hear it in the demo
10: Tonewheels0–2 s: one drawbar plays a sine tone.
20: Drawbars2–4 s: the same pitch uses all nine drawbars.
30: Envelope4–6 s: short notes use attack and release.
40: Vibrato6–9 s: a chord uses vibrato together. The melody continues from 9–12 s.

In the song, organ plays the chords. The drawbars change between sections, and vibrato starts after the first section.
